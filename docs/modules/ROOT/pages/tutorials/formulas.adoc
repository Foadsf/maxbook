= Computing with formulas
:description: Inline and display formulas, templates, compute in place, definitions and evaluated formulas.

Formulas in Maxbook are written visually, like in a word processor's equation editor, and Maxima understands them.
This page shows how to write them quickly and the three ways to compute with them.

== Inline and display formulas

* Type `$` in your text for a formula *inside a sentence*.
* Type `$` again in an empty formula, or press kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+M], for a *display formula* on its own line.
* kbd:[Ctrl+M] also starts an inline formula, and the format bar has buttons for both.

Leave a formula with kbd:[Esc], kbd:[Enter] (inline formulas), or kbd:[→] at its end.
Click a formula to edit it again.

== Templates: type a word

Inside a formula, type a word such as `int`, `frac`, `sum`, `prod`, `lim`, `sqrt`, `matrix`, `det`, `binom` or `abs`.
A list of matching templates opens:

image::formulas-search.webp[Typing int in a formula lists the Integral and Definite integral templates]

Press kbd:[Enter] or kbd:[Tab] to insert the highlighted template, kbd:[↑] and kbd:[↓] to choose another, or kbd:[Space] to keep the letters as they are.
A template has empty boxes; kbd:[Tab] moves from one to the next (the box you are in is highlighted).
Once no empty box is left, kbd:[Tab] does what it does in any formula: compute or leave it.

While you edit a display formula, the line under it shows the Maxima input the formula stands for:

image::formulas-display.webp[An integral being edited, with its Maxima translation integrate(x^2, x) under it]

If the formula is not complete yet, the line says what is missing instead (for example "integral is missing its differential (dx)"), so you always see what will be evaluated before anything runs.
The rules are listed in xref:reference/formula-syntax.adoc[Formula syntax].

NOTE: Some letter pairs are left alone on purpose: `gt` stays g·t and `in` stays i·n, because in a notebook they are usually products of two variables.
Function names (`sin`, `ln`, `exp`...) and Greek letters (`alpha`, `pi`...) still turn into their symbols as you type.

== Three ways to compute

=== 1. Compute in place: `=` then Tab

End a formula with `=` and press kbd:[Tab].
Maxima's value appears right after it, highlighted:

image::formulas-sum-typing.webp[The Basel sum, written with the sum template, ending in an equals sign]

image::formulas-sum.webp[The result pi squared over six appears after the sum]

Asking for a value means "work it out": integrals, derivatives, limits and sums written as formulas are evaluated (Maxima's `nouns` and `simpsum`), which is why the infinite sum above gives π²/6 rather than the sum again.
If Maxima cannot translate or evaluate the formula, a ⚠ appears instead; hover it to read the message.

=== 2. Definitions: `:=` then Tab

A formula with `:=` defines something in Maxima:

* `a := 3` defines a variable (Maxima's `a: 3`),
* `+f(x) := x^2 \sin x+` defines a function.

A green ✓ confirms the definition.
Everything below can use it: other formulas, Maxima blocks and plots.

=== 3. Evaluated display formulas: Shift+Enter

In a display formula, kbd:[Shift+Enter] turns it into an *evaluated* formula: Maxima runs it like a Maxima block and shows the result below it, with its label.

image::formulas-evaluated.webp[An evaluated display formula with the result x cubed over 3]

The button at the top right of the block switches between *Evaluated* and *Display only*.
A display-only formula is just typeset mathematics: nothing is sent to Maxima.

== Run all

*Run all* (or kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+Enter]) goes through the document from the top and recomputes, in order:

* every Maxima block,
* every display formula marked *Evaluated*,
* every formula ending in `=` or containing `:=`.

It stops at the first error and scrolls to it.

== Tips

* kbd:[Ctrl+K] searches all templates, also from outside a formula.
* A formula ending in `=` that you do not want computed is fine: it only computes when you press kbd:[Tab] in it or use *Run all*.
* In formulas, `e` is Euler's number, `\pi` is π, and a plain `i` is a variable; use the template search for the imaginary unit (`imag`).
* Use a Maxima block for anything that is not one expression: loops, several statements, options such as `ratprint: false`.
